Decision brief · D-001 · 2026-11-22 · revised

The feed sorts what deserves your attention into three buckets

For small startup teams with mixed roles who use AI heavily. The top bucket is yours to shape; the other two follow fixed rules that every item explains.

Needs you

Accept this as the product hypothesis? It holds everything we settled: the three buckets, the starting rules, the channel dial, what Focus means for a channel or thread, and the sender's Flag and Info.

What lands where on day one

An item lives in exactly one bucket. Focus wins, then Needs you. Each item says why it is there. The examples are from a seeded team.

Focus · your rules

Two starting rules, both removable. Add more in one click from any person, channel, thread or message.

  • Direct messages from people.
    “Anna: can you look at the pricing page before 3?”
    Direct message from Anna
  • Flagged messages that mention you, from a person or an agent.
    “Release agent: staging is ready. Approve the deploy to production?”
    Flagged for you by Release agent
Needs you · fixed rules

Directed at you, but not flagged for you.

  • Direct mentions of you.
    “@you does this copy work for the launch email?”
    Mentioned you in #launch
  • Replies in threads you started or replied in.
    “Marco replied: the numbers are from last week.”
    Reply in your thread
  • Flagged messages that mention nobody, in your threads and channels.
    “Build agent: who can approve the new API key?”
    Flagged for everyone in #infra
For your information · fixed rules

About you or your work. Nobody is waiting.

  • Reactions to your messages, grouped.
    “Priya, Marco and 3 others reacted to your update.”
  • Mentions of a whole channel or group.
  • Activity in threads you only follow.
  • Messages marked Info that concern you.

Never in the feed: agent text that is still streaming in, and ordinary channel traffic you were not addressed in.

Tuning it: one dial per channel

The starting rules are defaults. Each channel has a single dial, changed in one click from the channel.

LevelWhat reaches your feed from the channel
FocusEvery new top-level message and everything addressed to you, in Focus.
All activityThe same items, with the unaddressed ones in For your information.
DefaultThe starting rules above.
MutedOnly what names you: direct mentions and flags that mention you.

Whatever the level, a direct mention or a flag that names you always gets through, and the item says so: “#launch is set to All activity”.

What Focus means for a channel and for a thread

People focus a channel when they are on call, in a crunch, when every post matters, or when they are the one who answers. All of them want every post, not only what names them.

A channel in Focus
  • Every new top-level message lands in Focus, plus everything addressed to you there.
  • Replies in threads you are not part of stay out, so one busy thread cannot flood it.
  • Shown as one row: “#incidents · 6 new”, which expands. The count updates in place.
A thread in Focus
  • Every new reply lands in Focus, also as one row.
  • For work handed to an agent, that means its results and flags. Progress updates stay one bucket down.

Reactions never land in Focus. An end date on a Focus rule was considered and set aside for now.

How an item finds its bucket

  1. It appears at once, placed by exact rules

    First match wins: one of your Focus rules, then the fixed Needs you rules, then For your information. This step involves no model and no waiting.

  2. Later, a text rule may move it up

    If you wrote a rule in plain words (“anything about pricing or contracts”), a language model reads the item afterwards. It can only promote an item into Focus, never hide or lower one. If the model is slow or down, the item simply stays where step one put it.

    Built last, after an experiment

  3. It changes without jumping

    An edit or a delete never changes the size or position of what is on screen. A longer edit sits behind an expander, a deleted message leaves a marker in its place, and emptied space gets an illustration, not a gap.

What the sender can say: Flag and Info

One control with three positions, the same for people and agents. Almost every message carries neither.

Flag

“This needs attention.” Stronger than a mention, and not always a request.

Nothing

The feed's rules decide.

Info

“For the record, do not disturb anyone.” Never lands higher than For your information, even if it mentions you or the channel is in Focus. No notification.

Who a flag is for follows from the message itself.

in a direct conversationThe other personFocusThey respond
that mentions peopleEach person mentionedFocusEach responds, for themselves

The sender can withdraw a flag at any time.

Agents: always

An agent working on a task sometimes has to stop for a human: an approval, a choice between two options, a missing detail. Whenever it stops, it flags that message. Finished progress updates are Info, results carry no level, and text still streaming in never reaches the feed.

People: when they choose

A person can flag a message when a mention is not strong enough, or mark it Info for a note or a courtesy mention. Most messages carry neither. An AI suggesting a flag is a later idea, outside this scope.

In the proof of concept the agents in the seeded team follow a script (post, work, stop and ask) so a demo runs the same way every time. Whether a real model drives them is decision D-002.

Risks, and how we would notice
  • A rule puts something important too low. We lower, never hide, and each item shows its reason.
  • A broad rule floods Focus (“everything in #launch”). The product shows how many items the rule would have pulled in before you save it.
  • Flags are overused, like the high-importance flag in email. A flag closes when answered; one that mentions nobody lands a bucket lower and closes for everyone at the first response; the starting rule can be removed.
  • A text rule gets it wrong in the top bucket, where a mistake costs the most trust. Promote-only, the rule is named on the item, and an experiment comes before the build.

What early access should show: people clear the feed daily; how many add a Focus rule; how many start from Focus; items in higher buckets get acted on clearly more often.

The options that were set aside

B. People and agents as teammates. The strongest AI story, but the result depends on how good the agents are, and it pulls effort from messaging, the feed and reliable delivery.

C. A Slack-style feed, done carefully. The smallest scope, but early access would teach us nothing.
