Experiment · E-001 · 2026-11-22

The feed rules hold together, with one hole and a short list to confirm

I wrote the accepted rules as a runnable model, played 92 situations through it, and ran ten working days of a 30-person, 6-agent team. Nothing the decisions promise broke. One thing can never leave the feed.

Since decided

The hole is closed and six of the seven points are settled (decisions D-011 and D-013). A flag now has no state of its own: one that names you is kept for you until you press Done or respond. Rerun with that change, the Needs you list stays flat at about 3.5 rows instead of growing to 7.2. Still open: what sends a notification (D-012). The rest of this page shows the first run, which is the evidence for those decisions.

The verdict

Holds
  • 92 of 92 scripted situations behave as the decisions say.
  • 0 broken promises in about 150,000 simulated events: one item in one place, Info never above For your information, what names you always arrives, no marker for something never seen.
  • Focus stays short: about one row in nine of what a single list would hold.
Does not hold
  • A hole A flag nobody answers never leaves.
  • 6 to confirm Cases the decisions leave open or state in a way that surprises.
  • Not decided What sends a notification.

The hole: a flag nobody answers

We gave a flagged row one way out, the flag closing. In the simulated team 19 flags that named nobody and 18 that named a person were never answered in ten days. They stay.

The same happens in Focus for flags that name you and that you will not answer: one person reached 17 rows under “waiting on you”. Today a person has no way to say “not mine”.

  • Agents write a third of the messages and cause 0.2 of the 4.4 Focus rows a day. Without Info, agent items in Needs you rise from about 1,100 to 4,700 over the run.
  • The on-call person with two channels in Focus gets 17.5 Focus rows a day but meets 2.7 at a visit, because a burst of 15 alerts is one row.
  • Your first idea, mentions starting in Focus, would give 7.1 Focus rows a day instead of 4.4. Not a flood either way.
  • Needs you is the long bucket, not Focus. 79% of its items are thread replies, and 36% come from threads the person only replied in once. At double the traffic a person meets 10 rows on average and 24 on a bad visit.

Smaller behaviours I assumed; object to any you disagree with
  • Any reply in the thread by the flagged person answers the flag, not only a reply to that exact message. An Info reply does not answer it. An agent's reply does.
  • In a direct conversation, any later message from the flagged person answers the flag.
  • A flag with only a whole-channel mention counts as naming nobody. A flag that mentions only an agent reaches no person.
  • A direct message from an agent lands in Needs you, since the Focus starting rule says “from people”.
  • A Focus rule for a person lifts what already reaches you from them. It does not pull in their other messages.
  • If you remove a starting Focus rule, those items land in Needs you.
  • New activity on a kept row shows inside the Kept section.
  • Reactions to your message follow the thread: none when the thread is Off or the channel Muted.
  • Deleting your own message does not remove you from its thread.
  • A flag withdrawn from a message that still mentions you: if you had read it, the row stays in Focus until Done, marked “flag withdrawn”.
  • Replying in a thread you set to For your information by hand leaves it there.
  • A whole-channel mention inside a thread reply reaches nobody extra.
  • Reading a conversation in the channel itself marks its feed row read. Mark all read leaves open flags and kept rows.
  • Not modelled and not decided: where the sender of a flag sees who has answered, and how they withdraw it. I would put both on the message itself.

Each has a code (R03 to R33) in the experiment, with how often it came up.

How far to trust this

Checked independently A second agent, given only the decision records and the code, probed the model with its own scripts. It found six places where my model contradicted the decisions, eleven behaviours it had decided silently, and two skews in the simulation. All were corrected before these numbers were taken.

Not verified The corrections were not checked by a second independent pass. The traffic rates are assumptions: agents wrote 35% of messages here, below the 50% reported for Slack. People in the simulation read and reply at fixed odds. Text rules judged by a model are not in it. It says nothing about performance or delivery.
