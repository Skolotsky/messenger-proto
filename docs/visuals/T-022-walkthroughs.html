Seven walk-throughs: what happens, step by step

Each one follows a single event through the parts on the system map, and names the failure a test will inject to prove the claim. The numbers (two seconds, five seconds, three tries) are proposals for D-007.

The seven, and what each one shows

#Walk-throughWhat it shows
1A message is sentOne save, then search, the feed, a notification and an agent each get it on their own.
2Edited to add a mention, then deletedThe newly named person is told as if new; after the delete each reader is left with what D-004 says.
3A receiver is down and recoversNothing is lost, nobody else is delayed, and the affected screen says so.
4The same change twice, and out of orderA repeat changes nothing; a late edit cannot bring back a deleted message.
5A screen loses its connectionIt catches up from the last number it saw and ends equal to a fresh load.
6A dial is changedUnread rows are judged again; read rows stay; the list does not move while viewed.
7An agent streams a reply and stops with a flagText in progress travels a fast path and reaches no receiver; the finished message travels like any other.

1 · A message is sent

Anna posts in #launch: "@Marco can you sign off the pricing page? @Release agent hold the build until he does."

  1. Anna's screen shows it at once

    Marked "sending", and sent with an ID the screen made up. If the answer is lost and the screen tries again, the same ID means it is saved once.

  2. Conversations saves the message and the log entry together

    The message is version 1; the log gets change 4812. Either both are saved or neither. Anna's screen gets "saved".

    Test: stop the program right after the save. After a restart the change is in the log and is delivered.

  3. Screens with #launch open get it live

    As numbered update 4812, within a second.

  4. The search worker hands it to the search index

    The whole message with its version. It can be found within about two seconds.

  5. The feed works out where it lands for each person

    Marco is mentioned: a row in Needs you, "Anna mentioned you in #launch". Anna gets nothing (her own message). Others get a row only if their dial for #launch says so. In the same step the feed notes in its own log: "notification due for Marco".

  6. The notification sender waits five seconds, then sends once

    The entry appears in Marco's notification app. If Marco had #launch open in front of him, the feed already knew: his screen had reported "watching #launch", which is on the log too. Then the message is read on arrival: no row and no notification (D-015, rule 6).

  7. The release agent receives "you were mentioned"

    It reads events from its own place in its own stream, and confirms by number. An agent that was away reads on from where it stopped.

2 · The message is edited to add a mention, then deleted

Marco has read the row. Anna adds "@Lena" to the message. Lena has not looked yet when Anna deletes it.

  1. The edit is saved as version 2

    Change 4830 carries the whole new message. In #launch the text changes in place and shows "edited".

  2. Search replaces what it had

    Version 2 is newer than version 1. The old wording is no longer found (D-004).

  3. The feed compares whom the message addresses now and before

    Lena is new: she gets a row and a notification exactly as if she had been named at the start. Marco's row shows the new text and "edited"; it does not become unread again.

  4. The delete is saved as version 3

    Change 4855 carries only which message and its version. In #launch nothing remains, because the message had no replies; people looking at it right now keep the space until they move on.

  5. Search removes it and remembers "deleted at version 3"

    So a late copy of version 2 cannot bring it back.

  6. The feed leaves each reader what they had earned

    Marco had read it: his row keeps its place among read rows as "Anna deleted this message". Lena had not seen it: her row goes without a trace and her count drops. The feed knows which is which because reading is on the same numbered log as the delete, so "had she read it at that moment" has one answer.

  7. The notification follows

    If Lena's notification was still inside its five seconds, it is withdrawn and never sent. If it had gone out, it cannot be recalled; her entry now reads "message deleted".

    Test: play E-001's cases "edit adds a mention" and "delete of a row read, unread and kept" through the real parts and compare what each person is left with.

3 · A receiver is down for a while and recovers

The search index is switched off from the control panel for ten minutes while the team keeps talking.

  1. The search worker's call gets no answer

    It gives each call three seconds, then tries again after 1, 2, 4 seconds and so on, up to once a minute. Its place in the log does not move. Nothing is skipped and nothing is thrown away, however long the outage lasts.

  2. Everything else carries on

    Messages are saved and shown live. The feed and notifications have their own workers and their own places, so they are not delayed.

    Test: stop search during a burst and measure that the feed is as fresh as before.

  3. A person sees an honest search box

    If the index cannot be reached: "Search is unavailable right now. Your messages are safe." If it answers but is behind: results, with "the last 6 minutes may be missing". The control panel shows "search: 214 changes behind".

  4. Search comes back

    The next try succeeds. The worker delivers the backlog in log order, so an edit and a delete of the same message arrive in the order they happened.

  5. Within about half a minute it is level again

    The notice disappears.

    Test: afterwards compare the whole index with the truth. They must be equal: every current text there, no deleted message.

  6. A different case: the receiver answers but refuses one change

    After three tries that one change goes to the parking place, shown in the control panel with the reason and a "try again", and the worker moves on. Until it is released the receiver is shown as not level. Three different changes refused in a row count as an outage instead: the worker pauses and parks nothing more. Later changes to the same message still apply correctly because each carries the whole message and its version.

  7. The same outage at the notification sender

    "Due" notes wait in order. One for a message deleted or read meanwhile is followed directly by its "withdrawn", and nothing is sent. The rest arrive late, once, marked "delayed" past ten minutes. If the feed itself is down, its screen shows the rows it had with "Catching up"; conversations, unread marks on channels and search are unaffected, and notifications wait with it. If the feed is up but cannot apply one particular change, it fences off the one conversation or reader that change touches, marks those rows "may be out of date" and carries on with everything else.

4 · The same change is delivered twice, and out of order

  1. Twice, at search

    The worker delivered the edit (version 2), search applied it, and the answer was lost on the way back. The worker cannot know, so it sends again. Search already has version 2, ignores the repeat and answers "fine".

  2. Twice, at the feed

    The feed stores the number of the last change it applied in the same step as the rows it changed, and takes only the one that follows. Change 4830 again: ignored, no second row, no second "notification due". Change 4832 before 4831: refused with "I am at 4830", and the worker sends from there. The feed is the one receiver that insists on order.

  3. Twice, at the notification sender

    Every notification has a fixed name made from the person, the row and the change that caused it. The sender keeps a list of names it has sent. A repeat has the same name and is not sent again. A "withdrawn" that overtakes its "due" is remembered, so the late "due" sends nothing.

  4. Out of order: the edit arrives after the delete

    Search knows the message was deleted at version 3. Version 2 is older, so it is ignored. The message stays deleted. A delete beats any late edit.

  5. Why order can break at all

    Workers deliver in log order, but a change that was parked and released later, or a rebuild running beside live delivery, arrives late. Receivers are built not to depend on the order promise alone.

  6. An agent that gets an event twice

    The event has the same number both times, and the contract tells agents to ignore a number they have handled. What an agent posts carries an ID too, so an agent that crashed and repeats itself does not post twice.

    Test: put a troublemaker in front of every receiver that repeats each delivery and shuffles nearby ones. The end state must equal an undisturbed run.

5 · A screen loses its connection and catches up

Marco's phone is in a tunnel for two minutes.

  1. Every update a screen gets has a number

    Marco's screen last applied 4855 from conversations and update 97 of his own feed.

  2. The connection drops

    A quiet "Reconnecting" line appears. What is on screen stays. A message he writes now waits as "not sent" and is retried with the same ID.

  3. The connection returns

    The screen tries again after 1, 2, 4 seconds and so on, and says: "I have up to 4855, and feed 97."

  4. It receives everything after those numbers, in order

    And applies it the way it would have live: new feed rows are announced as "3 new", not inserted; edits and deletes keep their space.

  5. If it was away too long, it reloads

    Past a few hundred missed updates the answer is "too far behind", and the screen loads that view fresh from the current truth.

  6. A connection that looks alive but lost something

    About every twenty seconds the screen is told the latest numbers. One that sees a gap asks for what it missed.

    Test: cut the connection for thirty seconds while the script edits, deletes and reacts. The screen must end identical to one loaded fresh.

6 · A person changes a dial and their unread rows re-sort

After a burst of alerts, Lena sets #incidents from Default to Muted.

  1. The dial is saved as a fact about Lena

    In Conversations, with a numbered log entry like any other change. All her screens show the new level at once.

  2. Only the feed is told

    Search and agents are not sent what a person sets for themselves.

  3. The feed judges Lena's unread rows from #incidents again

    A flag that named nobody was in Needs you: it leaves. A reply in a thread she started stays (D-013). A direct mention stays. Rows she had already read are not touched.

  4. Each move is written to the feed's log

    A notification still inside its five seconds for a row that left is withdrawn.

  5. Her screen does not rearrange under her

    If the feed is open, the changes apply the next time she opens it (D-013). The counts update straight away.

    Test: E-001's dial and rule cases, played through the real parts: unread rows move, read rows never do.

7 · An agent streams a reply and then stops with a flag

The release agent answers in a thread and ends by asking Marco to approve.

  1. The agent says "I am starting a reply"

    Conversations saves an unfinished message and logs "reply started", with no text. Screens show the agent as working. Search, the feed and notifications ignore an unfinished message: it is never indexed, never a row, never a notification (D-001).

  2. The text arrives piece by piece on a fast path

    Pieces go straight to screens that have the thread open. They do not go through the log, so a reply of two hundred pieces is not two hundred deliveries to every receiver.

  3. A lost piece does not matter

    Conversations keeps the text so far. Each piece says where in the text it belongs; a screen that notices a gap, joins late or reconnects fetches the text so far and carries on.

  4. The agent finishes: "Build 2.4.1 is ready. @Marco approve the release?", flagged

    Conversations saves the full text as a finished message, version 2, one entry in the log.

  5. From here it travels like any message

    Search indexes it. The feed sees a flag that names Marco: Focus, kept for him, "Flagged by Release agent" (D-011). A notification is due and is sent after the five seconds.

  6. Marco reacts ✅ from the row

    The feed releases his kept row. The agent, shown as "waiting on Marco", receives "Marco reacted ✅ to your message" through its door and continues.

  7. If the agent dies mid-reply

    After a minute without a piece, or ten minutes in all (the limit Slack and Teams use), Conversations closes the message as it stands, labelled "stopped", through the log.

    Tests: kill the scripted agent mid-reply; cut a screen's connection mid-reply. Whether a stopped reply reaches anyone's feed is a small product question, listed in the draft.
