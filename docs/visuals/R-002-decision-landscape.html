Research · R-002 · 2026-11-22

How existing products do it, and what you need to decide first

The plumbing is the same in every product, so we borrow it. Your decisions are about what a person sees, what an edit or delete does, and how AI agents take part.

Needs you

Which decision do we open first? I recommend D-001 (scope and audience), then D-002 (the role of AI agents), because both change what every later decision has to cover.

The shape every product shares

Slack, Discord and Zulip all save the message first, record the change, and let the slow systems pick changes up on their own. Orange marks where the choices are yours.

What we can borrow and what we must choose

Same everywhere: borrow
  • Save first, then tell the other systems from a change record written in the same step.
  • Deliver at least once; receivers ignore repeats.
  • One queue per receiver, so a slow one blocks nobody.
  • Feed items come from mentions, thread replies and reactions, filtered by type.
  • Slack's own boundary: answer in 3 seconds, 3 retries, a unique ID per event.
Products disagree: you choose
  • An edit adds a mention: Zulip notifies, Mattermost does not, Slack does not say.
  • A message is deleted: no product documents what its feed does.
  • Clearing the feed: Slack clears, Linear snoozes, GitHub has Done and Save.
  • How fresh search and the feed must be, and what a person sees while one is down.
New with AI agents: you choose
  • Agents now sit in ordinary conversations (Slack and Teams, mid-2026), not a side panel.
  • They add live status, streamed replies and a “waiting on you” state.
  • Teams keeps in-progress output silent: only the finished message notifies.
  • Salesforce says half of Slack messages come from apps and agents, so the feed needs a noise rule.

The decisions, top-down

Each one gets its own short brief with options when we open it. “Lean” is where the evidence points today, not a decision.

#DecisionWhat the research saysLean
D-001
First		 Scope and audience. Who looks at this, how it is shown, and which surfaces exist: channels, threads, direct messages, switching between people. The brief asks for enough to exercise the feed, not a full product. Channels and threads, a busy seeded team, switch person without login. Needs your context first.
D-002
Second		 The role of AI agents. Are they full participants, how do status and streamed replies appear, and is the agent scripted or a real model? Slack, Teams and Linear treat agents as members you can mention. In-progress output is silent in Teams. Full participant with status, streamed replies and “waiting on you”; scripted so the demo is repeatable.
D-003 What the feed contains and how it is cleared. Item types, grouping, read and unread, the triage action. Mentions, thread replies, reactions; filters by type; one clear action; cleared threads return on new activity. Slack's types plus “an agent is waiting on you”; grouped reactions; read state and clear.
D-004 What an edit or delete does to feed items and notifications that already exist. Products disagree or stay silent. A sent notification cannot be recalled; an unsent one can be cancelled. The feed always shows the current truth; added mentions notify; a short send delay lets deletes cancel notifications.
D-005 System shape and stack. One program or several, the database, how live updates reach the screen. One source of truth, receivers behind real boundaries, numbered live updates with catch-up. One deployable with network-style boundaries inside, as the brief allows. Stack to be proposed.
D-006 The delivery promise. What each receiver is guaranteed about loss, repeats and order. Nobody promises exactly-once across a network. At-least-once with repeat-safe receivers is standard. Nothing lost, repeats harmless, order per message by version, deletes always win.
D-007 Quality targets. How fresh, how long we retry, what a person sees during an outage, how a receiver is rebuilt. Discord accepts a delay before search; Slack retries 3 times then gives up; GitHub does not retry. Numbers to set together, each backed by a test that breaks the receiver.
D-008 The contract for agents as receivers. What an agent is told, what it may read, and what stops agents triggering each other. Slack: explicit permissions, 3-second answer, retries. No official loop rule anywhere. Same contract as the other receivers, plus per-channel access. Depends on D-002.
