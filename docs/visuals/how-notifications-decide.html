Contexia · notification rules

How notifications decide

The feed waits for you. A notification interrupts you. So the rules are about one question: when is it worth breaking someone’s concentration? This page explains those rules, the five-second wait, and what happens afterwards. It is about behaviour, not about the program that delivers them.

Status

These rules come from the product decisions D-004, D-012, D-014, D-015 and D-034, and they are built in the Activity feed.

The whole thing in six rules

RuleWhy
1Only Focus and Needs you can notify. For your information never does.An interruption should mean “this is for you”.
2One notification per row, not per message.Six replies in a thread are one reason to look.
3A row can notify again after you read it and something new arrives, or if it moves up into Focus while unread.Those are new reasons to look.
4Wait five seconds first. If it stops being deserved in that time, nobody is interrupted.A notification cannot be taken back, while a typo or a quick delete is common.
5Count those seconds by the log’s time, never by a machine’s clock.A system running late must reach the same decision as a healthy one.
6Once sent, deliver it only once, and keep its entry truthful.It cannot be recalled, but it can stop being misleading.

The life of one notification

Who holds this logic

The Activity feed holds all of it. Everyone else either supplies facts or delivers the result.

Why the feed

Whether something deserves an interruption depends on buckets, dials, Focus rules and what the person has read. The feed is the only part that already knows all of these.

Why not the sender

It would need a second copy of the rules and of everyone’s read state. In production it would be an outside service we do not own.

The cost

If the feed is down, no new notification can be decided. They wait and go out late. A late notification was judged better than a wrong one.

Rule 1 · What is allowed to interrupt

A notification means a row just became unread in a bucket that notifies.

There are no separate notification settings per channel. The dials you already use for the feed decide, because they decide the bucket. That keeps one mental model: if something would land in Needs you, it can notify. If it would land in For your information, it stays quiet.

Default

Focus and Needs you notify.

“Focus only”

One personal switch. Only Focus notifies, so you hear about what you chose and what was flagged for you.

Left out on purpose

Quiet hours, sounds, email, digests and reminders. Each is real product work, and none is needed to test the idea.

Rule 4 · The five-second wait

Send nothing for five seconds, so that a mistake or a quick read can make the interruption unnecessary.

Notifications are the one thing in the product that cannot be undone. Once a phone has buzzed, the attention is already spent. And the first seconds after sending are exactly when people fix typos, delete the wrong message, or when the other person is already reading. A short wait removes those interruptions at a cost nobody notices.

What cancels it

During the holdWhat happens
You read the row, on any deviceCancelled
The message is deletedCancelled, unless something else unread on the row still deserves it
Your mention is edited away, or a reaction is taken backSame rule
You turn that channel or thread’s dial downSame rule: if nothing there still earns a notifying bucket, it is cancelled
The text is editedNot cancelled. It goes out with the latest text
The feed cannot apply a change in that conversation (a fence)Cancelled, because the row can no longer be trusted

Four situations, on a clock

Nothing happenssent at about 6 s

The hold passes, and the next clock tick in the log releases it.

You read it quicklynothing sent

This is the most common case the hold exists for.

Anna deletes itnothing sent

A deleted mistake stays deleted, and you never knew it happened.

Someone replied before the deletesent, about the reply

In a thread you started, Anna replies, Lena replies, and Anna deletes hers. The row is still unread and Lena’s reply still earns Needs you, so the notification survives and now speaks of Lena’s reply. It must never show the deleted text.

Rule 5 · Why the time comes from the log

This is the part that sounds technical, but the idea is simple. “Five seconds” must mean five seconds between the events, not five seconds on the clock of whichever program happens to be looking. Every change in the log has a timestamp. The feed handles changes in log order. So it always sees a read before it sees any time later than that read.

Counting on a machine’s clock
  1. 10:14:00 Anna mentions Marco
  2. 10:14:01 Marco reads it
  3. The feed is two minutes behind
  4. It reaches the mention at 10:16:00: “that is old, send it”

Marco is notified about something already read two minutes ago.

Counting on the log’s clock
  1. 10:14:00 Anna mentions Marco
  2. 10:14:01 Marco reads it
  3. The feed is two minutes behind, but takes the log in order
  4. It meets the read before any tick past 10:14:05

Cancelled. However late the feed is, the answer is the same.

The clock tick

One problem remains. If nothing else happens after a mention, the log goes silent and nothing would ever say “five seconds have passed”. So the log gets a small entry called a tick, about once a second while an organization is active, until one is stamped at least six seconds after the last real change. An idle organization gets no ticks and costs nothing.

Why “within 8 seconds”

5 s of hold, up to about 1.5 s for the next tick that is late enough, then delivery. That is the promise when everything is healthy.

The term

Time from the log is event time; time on a machine is processing time. A tick works as a watermark: “everything up to this moment has been seen”.

Rule 6 · After it is sent

A notification already delivered cannot be recalled. What we can do is make sure its entry never misleads. The notification app shows each entry in its current state: the edited text, “message deleted”, or read. This is the same principle the whole product follows: show the current truth.

Each notification has a fixed name, such as acme / marco / #launch:msg_7Q2 / 4812: the organization, the person, the row and the change that caused it. The sender remembers the names it has sent, so a repeated delivery changes nothing.
