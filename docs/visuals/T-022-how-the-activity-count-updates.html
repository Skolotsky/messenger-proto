How the number on "Activity" gets updated

The number is Marco's unread rows in Focus and Needs you. The screen never works it out: the feed keeps it, changes it in the same step as the row that caused the change, and it travels to every open screen of Marco's as a numbered update.

Going up: Anna mentions Marco, his badge goes from 9 to 10

  1. The message is saved with its log entry

    Nothing about Marco's feed happens here. Conversations only records what Anna did.

  2. A delivery worker hands the change to the feed

    Within tens of milliseconds. If the feed is down, the change waits; nothing is lost.

  3. The feed decides, and counts

    Marco is mentioned, so the conversation's row for him becomes unread in Needs you. His count goes from 9 to 10 in the same step, and the feed writes "update 98 for Marco". If the row had already been unread, the count would not change: it counts rows, not messages.

  4. The delivery worker nudges Conversations

    The feed answers the worker "applied"; the worker then tells Conversations "the feed has news for this organization". The nudge carries no data, so losing it loses nothing. The feed itself never calls Conversations.

  5. Conversations asks for Marco's updates after the last one it sent

    It gets update 98: the changed row and Marco's counts.

  6. Every open screen of Marco's gets update 98

    Phone and laptop alike, also with Activity closed. The badge shows 10. The screen does no counting.

Going down: Marco reads it

  1. Marco opens the conversation

    His screen shows 9 at once. It does not wait: this is his own action.

  2. "Read up to here" is saved, with its log entry

    The same path as a message: Conversations, the log, a delivery worker, the feed.

  3. The feed marks the row read and lowers the count

    Update 99 goes to all of Marco's screens. On the screen that did it, 99 confirms what it already shows; on his other device the badge drops now. The same read position clears the channel's unread mark in the sidebar, which Conversations works out by itself.

When something is slow or lost

What happensWhat the badge does
The nudge is lostConversations checks every 5 seconds for organizations with open screens, so the badge is at most that late.
Marco's connection dropsThe badge stays. On reconnecting the screen says "I have up to 97" and receives 98 and 99 in order.
The feed is down or far behindThe badge stays at its last value and Activity says "Catching up". Messages, channels and their unread marks keep working.
The same change is delivered to the feed twiceNothing: the feed takes only the change that follows the last one, so the count cannot go up twice.
The screen is loaded freshIt asks once for Marco's rows and counts, and for the number of the last update, then follows from there.

What "Conversations" is in this picture

One program with three jobs: it is the public address for screens and agents, it holds their open connections, and it does the conversation logic and owns the original data. It also passes a screen's questions on to the feed and to search, so a screen only ever talks to it.

Why one program

The copy that saves a message is the copy that holds the organization's connections, so it can push at once without any messaging between programs. Fewer parts, and nothing to keep in step.

What that costs

Restarting it for a change to the logic drops every open connection (screens reconnect and catch up by number). Connections and logic cannot be grown separately.

When to split them

When connections must stay up across releases, when one organization needs more connections than one copy holds, or when other services need to push to screens. Then a thin gateway holds connections and the address, and a shared in-memory channel sits between it and the logic.

Where the number lives

Kept by the feed

One count per reader and bucket, in the feed's own area of PostgreSQL, changed in the same transaction as the row. It can always be rebuilt from the rows, and the rows from the log.

Not worked out on the screen

A screen sees only some rows and may have missed updates. If it counted for itself, two devices could disagree. It shows the number it was sent.

With many organizations

Every step is inside one organization: its log, its rows, its copy of Conversations. Nothing here gets slower because other organizations exist.

How fast: the experiments measured 7 to 12 ms from save to the feed on a small build; the target the product is held to is 2 seconds, 19 times in 20 (G-013). The nudge and the 5-second check are part of the design and are not tried yet.
