Contexia · failure map

What can fail, and what covers it

Fourteen failures, from a copy of a program dying to the clock jumping backwards. The table shows what people see and what covers each one. Three are explained below, along with the one failure that is not covered.

Status

This page describes the designed behaviour. Timings come from the contracts, and each guardrail's current status is in docs/guardrails.md. The demonstration runs one copy of each program, and two copies run only in the tests, behind a small router.

Four ideas cover almost everything

1 · The truth is saved first

Apart from the live pieces of an agent's unfinished reply, nothing is pushed, delivered or answered before it is committed in PostgreSQL together with its log entry. Everything after that can be repeated from the log.

2 · Everyone keeps their own place

Each screen, each worker per receiver, each agent, the feed and the notification sender remember the last number they have. After any failure they ask "what comes after my number?".

3 · Repeating is harmless

Actions carry an ID. The feed takes only the next change. Search keeps the newest version. A notification name is sent once. So "send it again" is always safe.

4 · Whatever is held, is held with a time limit

A worker's claim, a copy's lease and a "watching" each end by themselves if their holder disappears. A held notification is saved and waits for the feed to return.

And programs keep nothing that matters in memory

Any copy can be killed. Connections are rebuilt from what screens and the database know, and an agent's per-minute count starts fresh.

A part that is behind says so

"Reconnecting", "Catching up", "may be out of date", "the last N minutes may be missing". A surface more than about 10 seconds behind says so.

1 · A copy of Conversations dies

Suppose two copies run, as they do in the tests. Each organization is served by one of them. Copy A, which serves acme, dies. Copy B needs nothing prepared: it holds no state about acme and learns everything from the screens and the database.

What people see

Screens reconnect by themselves. If that takes more than 10 seconds, they say "Reconnecting". What is on screen stays. A message typed meanwhile shows as waiting and is sent afterwards. Other organizations on copy B notice nothing.

What covers it

The screen reconnects by itself and states its last numbers. The router sends acme to a live copy. That copy reads the missing changes from the log. Waiting actions are sent again with the same IDs, so nothing is saved twice. The screen says again what is open in front of it. Agents ask again from their own place.

How long · guardrail

Level within 2 s of reconnecting. In the tests, the test itself moves acme. A production router would use health checks, and it is not built. Watchings held by A end within about 25 s. Guardrails: G-015 and G-027 (an organization moved between two copies mid-burst).

8 · The feed cannot apply one change

A fault in the feed’s own rules on one particular change. The feed cannot skip it (order matters) and must not stop everything.

What people see

Rows in that conversation, or that reader's rows in it, are marked "may be out of date". Every other row is exact.

What covers it

The change is set aside and a fence goes up around the one conversation, or the one reader in it. Later changes inside the fence are set aside too; notifications on hold inside it are dropped. A fault on an organization-wide change, or more than five fences, stops that organization’s feed and no other. Repair: fix the fault, rebuild that organization’s feed from the log.

How long · guardrail

Until the rebuild, which takes under 2 minutes for the seeded team. Guardrails: G-009 and G-020.

9 · The notification sender is down, or dies while sending

The sender stops for minutes, or dies between sending a notification and noting that it did.

What people see

A notification arrives late; more than 10 minutes late it is marked "delayed". Never one for something read within the 5-second hold. If you read it after that while the sender is down, it still arrives late, marked as read.

What covers it

Notes wait in the feed’s own log and are delivered in order. Each notification has a fixed name and a name is sent once; the list of sent names survives even a wipe of the sender. The feed runs the 5-second hold by timestamps in the log, so a read within the hold cancels it however late the feed is, and the sender is never told. With a real push provider the rule would be "rather twice than never", with the name as the provider’s duplicate key.

How long · guardrail

As long as the outage. Guardrails: G-010 (stopped 5 and 11 minutes, killed between sending and noting, wiped) and G-011.

14 · PostgreSQL is down: not covered

Every program survives the loss of a copy of itself, but all of them depend on the one database. If it stops, nothing can be saved or read until it is back. Nothing saved is lost, and everything resumes from its place afterwards, but the product is down meanwhile.

What people see

"Reconnecting", and messages that stay waiting.

The production answer

A standby database that confirms each write and takes over automatically, usually within tens of seconds. The design allows it; it is not built or tested here.

Also not built

The router in front of Conversations; a second database for spreading organizations; trimming of old log entries.

The last resort: rebuild

The feed, the search index and the notification sender hold copies, never originals. If one is damaged or its rules were wrong, it is emptied for one organization and filled again from that organization's log. The sender keeps its list of names already sent, so nobody is notified twice. Guardrail G-020: under 2 minutes for the seeded team, and the other organization untouched.
