Experiment · E-003 · 2026-11-22

The proposed delivery design, built small and broken on purpose

The design holds: under every failure we injected, nothing was lost, nothing was repeated visibly, and every receiver ended equal to the truth. It also showed that one promise in the proposal, about notifications, needed a different mechanism, which is now in.

What was built

A throwaway version of the system map: the core, the delivery workers, a search stand-in, the feed and a notification sender, as separate programs on this machine that talk only over the network. A scripted team of 12 posts, replies, mentions, flags, edits, deletes, reacts and reads at up to 300 actions a second. The test stops programs for real and then compares every receiver with the truth. 75 checks; all came out as expected.

Nothing lost

The core was killed five times mid-burst. Every action a screen was told "saved" is there, once.

One slow part delays nobody

With search stopped or answering too slowly, the feed stayed 7 ms behind (worst 18 ms). Search cleared 661 waiting changes 0.6 s after coming back.

Repeats and late copies change nothing

Half of all deliveries were disturbed: repeated, answered into the void, or delivered again late. End state identical.

Wipe and rebuild

The feed was wiped mid-burst and was level again 1.4 s later, equal to a clean replay. The notification tray refilled to the same 565 entries; nobody was notified twice.

The finding that matters: when is a notification held back?

160 mentions were read or deleted 0.3 s later, inside the hold, so none of them should notify. How many went out anyway, by how the hold is measured:

from when the note reached the sender (what the proposal said)8 of 160147 of 160fails
by the log's own clock: sent only once something stamped later than the end of the hold has arrived through the log0 of 1600 of 160holds

The third way needs one small addition: while there is activity the core writes a clock tick into the log about ten times a second, and the feed passes it on. The sender then knows "everything up to this moment has reached me". With it the promise "no notification for something read or deleted within the hold" is true however late any part is.

Two smaller findings

Time must not run backwards in the log

This machine's clock steps back by about 0.8 s now and then (measured). Before the core forced its timestamps never to decrease, 4 of 617 notifications in a healthy run went out wrongly. Parts compare log times only with log times, never with their own clock.

An idle receiver must be able to say where it is

A wiped notification sender went unnoticed while nothing new arrived. The worker's idle check-in is now answered with "I am at 0", and the worker starts over.

What this does not show

  • Not verified The database. The experiment uses SQLite, which lets one program write at a time. The proposal's PostgreSQL has a known trap (a change saved a moment late being stepped over) that cannot occur here. It was only modelled. Checking it for real needs PostgreSQL, which would have to be downloaded.
  • Real speeds. 6 to 12 ms is this machine, local calls, a tiny store. It says the 2-second target has a wide margin, not what the product will measure.
  • The feed's rules. A subset was used; E-001 is the evidence for the rules themselves.
  • Screens and agents. No browser, no live connection, no streamed replies.
