Contexia: the architecture, for review

Everything a person or an agent does is saved in one place and written to a numbered log in the same step. Search, the feed and notifications are each fed from that log by their own worker, so one of them being slow or down delays only itself and loses nothing.

Where it stands

All technical decisions are taken. You decided the shape, the database and the live connection (D-005; a message broker was tried and dropped again, D-027 and D-028), Go for the server (D-025), who owns what a reader did (D-017), who decides a notification (D-018), and that the architecture is ready for many organizations (D-026, section 11). On your delegation I decided the delivery promise (D-006), the numbers (D-007) and the agent door (D-008). Any of them can be overturned.

An organization is the tenant; the seeded team lives inside one. There is no organization switcher in this scope: the second organization exists only for the tests. How the system grows to many organizations by adding copies and databases: D-029.

1 · The parts and what moves between them

2 · Three ideas carry the whole design

1 · Save and log in one step

A message and the note "this changed" are saved together or not at all. That closes the gap where a message is saved and nobody is told. Established practice (the transactional outbox).

2 · Deliver again rather than lose

A worker that is not sure a change arrived sends it again. Each change carries the whole current message and its version, so a receiver ignores a repeat or an older copy, and a delete beats any late edit.

3 · The feed is worked out, never typed in

The feed holds no facts of its own. It is computed from the log: messages, and what each reader did (read, dials, rules, Keep). Wipe it and it can be rebuilt to the same state.

4 · The contracts: what crosses each boundary

A contract says what is sent, what is promised and what happens on failure. These become files under docs/contracts/ once the decisions behind them are accepted. Payload examples are in sections 8 and 10.

Screen → Conversations

what a person does
Carries
Send, edit, delete, react, read up to here (opening a conversation, or Mark read on a row), watching this conversation, Keep, Done, a dial, a Focus rule, a Focus person removed or added back, the "Focus only" switch, Undo. Each with an ID the screen chose. And one question: "what would this change have moved in the last 7 days?" (D-020).
Promise
Answered "saved" only after it is saved and logged. The same ID again changes nothing.
On failure
The screen keeps the action as "not sent" and retries with the same ID.
Evidence
E-003: core killed five times mid-burst; every acknowledged action saved once.

Conversations → Screen

live updates
Carries
Numbered updates for what the screen shows: conversations, the person's feed rows, text of a reply being written.
Promise
Within a second when connected. After any gap the screen ends equal to a fresh load.
On failure
"Resume after number N", or "too far behind: reload".
Evidence
E-005, in a real browser: connection cut five times, refused for 2 s, one update in twenty lost silently, away past the limit. The screen always ended equal to a fresh load. Two screens broken on purpose ended wrong. 12 tabs at once: all connected.

Conversations → Search index

Carries
Message created, changed, deleted. The whole current message and its version; a delete carries only which message and its version.
Promise
Never lost; may repeat; in log order, though search does not rely on order.
On failure
No answer in 3 s or an error: wait, retry, skip nothing. "I refuse this one": parked after three tries, shown, released by hand.
Evidence
E-003: stopped, slowed, refusing, wiped; always ended equal to the truth.

Conversations → Activity feed

the strict one
Carries
Every change, including what readers did and clock ticks, each with its number and the number before it.
Promise
Never lost; strictly in order; in effect once.
On failure
Out of sequence: the feed answers "I am at N" and the worker continues from there. A change it cannot apply: set aside, with a fence around the conversation or reader it touches; later changes inside the fence are set aside too. More than five fences: it stops, because that is not one bad change.
Evidence
E-003: killed, wiped mid-burst, its worker's place moved by mistake; always equal to a clean replay. With two faults planted: 51 rows fenced, the other 452 exact.

Activity feed → Notification sender

Carries
"Due", "changed", "withdrawn" and clock ticks from the feed's own numbered log. A due note has a fixed name, the person, the reason and the text. The sender's list is shown in the separate mock notification app (D-022), which reads the stand-in directly, not through the product.
Promise
Never lost; a name is sent once; nothing is sent for what was read or deleted within the hold.
On failure
Same waiting and retrying. Late notifications are sent late, marked "delayed" past ten minutes.
Evidence
E-003: 0 of 160 wrongly sent with the feed down or overloaded; 565 entries before a wipe, the same 565 after.

Conversations ↔ Agents

the agent door
Carries
To the agent: numbered events that address it. From the agent: a message with a level, a streamed reply (start, pieces, finish), a status.
Promise
Events kept seven days, in order, possibly repeated with the same number. A post with the same ID is saved once.
On failure
An agent that is away reads on from its place. A reply with no new piece for a minute is closed as "stopped". An agent posting too fast is told to slow down and sends the same thing again later.
Evidence
E-003 with scripted agents: one away for 3 s, all crashing now and then before noting their place. 183 events, none missed, in order; 46 repeated replies saved once; 59 replies written piece by piece, none seen by a receiver before it was finished; 10 abandoned ones closed as "stopped"; two agents answering each other stopped after three.

5 · D-005: the shape, the database, the live connection

The brief allows the search index and the notification sender to be stand-ins inside one program, and asks that their boundaries be treated as real. The question is how real we make them.

A. One program

Search, notifications and the feed are sections of the same program. "Slow" and "down" are a switch inside it.

Upside
The least to build and run. The brief permits it.
Risk
The boundary is a convention; a shortcut across it goes unnoticed. An outage is acted: nothing can really be stopped.
Undo
Moderate.

B. Parts that talk only over the network

Decided

One codebase. Conversations, the feed, the two stand-ins and the scripted team each run as their own program and reach each other only by network calls. One command starts them all.

Upside
A receiver can really be stopped, slowed or wiped while you watch, and the tests do exactly that.
Risk
Five programs to start and watch; slower tests. Kept in check by one start command and a status view in the control panel.
Undo
Cheap toward A or C.

C. B plus a message broker

later, if needed

A dedicated product (Kafka, NATS or similar) carries the changes between Conversations and the receivers.

Upside
What large products run.
Risk
One more system to run and explain, and it removes none of the hard parts: changes still have to get into it safely and receivers still see repeats. At 1,500 messages a day it adds nothing we can show.
Undo
Moderate.
Comes with itRecommendedAlternativeWhy

6 · D-017: who owns what a reader did

Where a message lands depends on the reader: their Focus rules, dials, what they read, what they kept. Someone has to own those.

A. Conversations owns the facts; the feed is worked out

Decided

Read, dial, rule, Keep and Done are saved in Conversations like a message is, and travel on the same numbered log. The feed computes rows from the log and holds nothing original.

Upside
D-004 asks "had the reader read it when it was deleted?". With reads and deletes in one numbered order there is exactly one answer, on every rebuild. Unread marks on channels and the feed use the same read position (D-015). The feed can be wiped and rebuilt.
Risk
What you do in the feed goes round through the log before the feed knows it (under a second). The screen shows your own action at once and does not wait.
Undo
Expensive.

B. The feed owns reader state

The screen writes reads, dials and Keep straight to the feed. Conversations knows only messages.

Upside
The feed answers at once; less on the log.
Risk
The feed now holds originals: wiping it loses what people did. Read state has two homes (channels need it too). The order of "read" against "deleted" depends on which call arrived first.
Undo
Expensive.

The look-and-feel decisions added to what a reader does: removing a person from Focus and adding them back, the option that adds everyone who writes to you directly (D-021), Mark read on a row, Undo (D-020, D-023). The list of Focus people fills itself, so it is something worked out, like a thread's level: under A the feed works it out from the log and a rebuild gives the same list; under B the feed would have to keep it as an original.

Consequences of A: the feed keeps its own copy of what it needs (messages, who took part in which thread, each reader's settings), so it never has to ask Conversations for anything. The effective level of a thread dial, the reason on a row, the counts and the four history tabs are all answered by the feed. A person's screen also reports which conversation is open in front of them; that is a fact on the log too, so "arrived while they were watching" (D-015, rule 6) is known when the message is judged and makes no row and no notification. It ends when the screen's connection drops. Later, a language model's verdict on a text rule is handed to Conversations and saved as a fact, so a rebuild does not ask the model again; if the model is slow or fails, the row stays where it is.

7 · D-018: who decides that a notification is due

The brief lists the notification sender beside the feed, as an independent system that receives messages. D-012 says a notification is sent when a feed row becomes unread in Focus or Needs you, once per row. So the sender needs to know something only the feed knows.

A. The feed decides; the sender delivers

Decided

When a row becomes unread in a bucket that notifies, the feed writes "due" to its own numbered log. A worker carries it to the sender with the same promise as any other change. Later it may write "changed" or "withdrawn".

Upside
The rule lives in one place. The feed and the notification app cannot disagree about what deserved an interruption.
Risk
The sender stands behind the feed, not beside it: while the feed is down, notifications wait. This departs from a literal reading of the brief and the proposal says so.
Undo
Moderate.

B. The sender gets messages and applies the rules itself

Three receivers side by side, as the brief draws it.

Upside
The sender does not depend on the feed.
Risk
The feed's rules exist twice, need every reader's dials and read state twice, and will drift. "One per row" cannot be told from messages alone.
Undo
Moderate.

C. A, and deletes also go straight to the sender

The sender additionally hears "message deleted" from Conversations directly.

Upside
A deleted message is not announced even while the feed lags.
Risk
Two sources to reconcile for a rare case; while the feed lags, the "due" is waiting behind it anyway.
Undo
Cheap.

Best practice: Slack's 2026 notification rebuild separates deciding what deserves attention from delivering it (R-002). A is that separation.

8 · D-006: what each receiver is promised

A. Send once and hope

After saving, tell each receiver; if it fails, move on.

Upside
Trivial.
Risk
Every outage loses changes for good. Search shows deleted messages forever.
Undo
Cheap.

B. Never lost, possibly repeated, in order

Decided

A worker per receiver reads the log from its own place, in order. Outage: wait and resume, without limit. A refused change: park it, move on. Receivers ignore repeats and older versions.

Upside
A promise that can be stated in one sentence and proven by breaking things.
Risk
One change the receiver chokes on could stall it; hence the parking place. Telling "down" from "refuses this one" has to be exact.
Undo
Expensive: every receiver is built on it.

C. Each change on its own, a few retries, then dropped

How Slack's Events API treats outside apps: three retries, no order.

Upside
A bad change never holds up others.
Risk
Order is lost routinely and a longer outage loses changes; needs a separate repair job.
Undo
Moderate.

"Exactly once" is not on the list: nobody can promise it across a network (R-002). The honest version is "at least once, and a repeat changes nothing".

Upside
The best-known form.

11 · D-026: ready for many organizations (multi-tenant), and how it scales

Decided You asked for the architecture to be ready for many organizations in one installation, and scalable because of it. The rule that delivers both: organizations are completely independent of each other. The organization is part of every key and every contract, each organization has its own numbered log, and the database itself keeps organizations apart.

How it grows is worked out on its own page (D-029): growing by adding. In short: organizations are spread over databases through a small directory; programs without memory (workers, the feed) are multiplied freely; programs with memory (the live connection, the agent door) each serve the organizations routed to them; one very large organization is the limit that remains.

Tried on PostgreSQL (E-004, second part)

Eight organizations, two writers each (the test code calls them teams). One organization keeps every save open for 300 ms. With one log for everybody, a save in the other organizations took up to 2.6 s; with a log per organization, at most 34 ms. Nothing was stepped over (0 of 3,200). A log per organization buys independence, not more speed from one database. (That test saved about 1,000 changes a second, which was the limit of its test program; with separate writer programs the same database saved about 9,500, E-007.)

What a failure now touches

A receiver that is down is down for every organization. Everything else is per organization: a change that is parked, three refusals that pause delivery, a fence, a feed that stops after five fences, a rebuild, a reset. A receiver has its own answer for "this organization is stopped", so the others carry on.

Not built

Creating or administering organizations, sign-in, limits per organization, one identity across organizations, several copies of a program actually running. The first version runs one organization with the seeded team in it, and has no organization switcher; a second small organization exists only so the tests can prove separation.

Tried since (E-006, last part): with one organization holding 20,000 waiting changes, our own worker taking organizations in turn applied the others' changes in 25 ms (worst 61 ms); with that rule taken out, 8 s (worst 25 s).

Two guardrails come with it: nothing of one organization is ever shown to or changed by another (enforced by the database itself, G-025), and what happens in one organization does not delay another (G-026). The diagrams and examples earlier on this page show one organization.

13 · Risks and trade-offs in one place

Risk or trade-offWhat would happenWhat the design doesWhat remains
Notifications depend on the feed (D-018)The feed is down: no notifications until it is back.They are held, then sent late, once, marked "delayed". The rule for what notifies lives in one place.A departure from the brief's picture of three receivers side by side. Yours to accept or not.
A change the feed cannot applyA fault in the feed's rules on one change. Stopping would take the whole feed down for everyone; skipping would leave it quietly wrong.Neither: the change is set aside and a fence goes up around the one conversation, or the one reader, it touches. Those rows are marked "may be out of date"; later changes inside the fence wait with it. Everything outside stays exact. After the fix the feed is rebuilt from the log.Inside a fence nothing new notifies, and a notification already on its way may still go out although the person had read the message (1 case in the experiment). If more than five fences go up, the feed stops: that is a broken feed, not a bad change.
One writer at a time for log numbersA slow save would hold up every other save.The number is taken as the very last step. Measured: about 1,000 saves a second either way.A ceiling far above this product's size; at very large size the log is split by conversation.
A parked changeSearch differs from the truth until someone presses "try again" (a deleted message stays findable).Shown in the control panel; the receiver counts as not level; a test proves both states.Needs a person. Acceptable for a refusal, which should be rare.
Telling an outage from a refusalMisread one way, a broken receiver parks everything; the other way, one bad change stalls it.Only an explicit "I refuse this one" parks; three in a row are treated as an outage.Depends on receivers answering honestly. A sent notification cannot be recalledSomeone is notified of a message deleted six seconds later.A five-second hold catches typo fixes and quick deletes; afterwards the entry shows "message deleted".Inherent. The hold length is yours (D-007).
Several programs instead of one (D-005)More to start, watch and explain for a prototype.One command starts all; the control panel shows each part's state; the tests run in this mode.Slower tests. The price of outages that are real.
A row is written per reader for every postAt thousands of people, one post in a big channel means thousands of writes.Nothing at this size (channels of 30).A known shortcut; large channels would be worked out when the feed is read.
The log is kept forever and a rebuild replays all of itRebuild time grows with history.Two weeks of a 36-member team is small. E-003 replayed 2,400 changes in under two seconds with a subset of the rules.At the seeded team's size: 80 s on the test build with a subset of the rules. The screen's catch-up is our ownA fault there shows as a stale or wrong screen after a dropped connection.Numbered updates, a "too far behind: reload" answer, a test that cuts the connection mid-burst.Not tried yet.
The rule against agents setting each other off is oursToo strict: agents cannot cooperate. Too loose: a loop floods a channel.A cap of three agents in a row and 20 messages a minute, both settings.No vendor documents a rule to copy. Needs watching in use.
Many organizations share one copy of each part and one database (D-026)If the database is lost, every organization's data is. A receiver that is down is down for every organization.Organizations are independent, so parts and databases can later be split by organization. One organization's burst, slow save or fence does not touch another.Several copies and several databases are not built. One very large organization is not helped by splitting by organization.

14 · Evidence: what was tried, and what was not

E-003 · the design, built small and broken on purpose

The core, workers, search, feed and sender as separate programs; a scripted team at up to 300 actions a second. Programs were killed mid-burst, search was stopped, slowed and made to refuse, half of all deliveries were repeated or delivered late, and each receiver was wiped. 75 checks, all as expected: nothing lost, a slow part delayed nobody, every receiver ended equal to the truth. Four versions broken on purpose were each caught. Results.

E-004 · the log on PostgreSQL

16 writers saving 4,000 changes at once while a worker follows the log. With ordinary numbering the worker never saw 919 to 948 of 4,000. With numbering one writer at a time: 0, at the same speed (about 1,000 saves a second). A worker woken by the database has the change 1 ms after it is saved.

E-005 · the screen's side, in a real browser

A page that follows the numbered-update contract, with its connection cut five times, refused for two seconds, losing one update in twenty, and away past the limit: always equal to a fresh load. Three scripted agents, one away for three seconds, all crashing now and then: no event missed, every repeated reply saved once, text in progress never seen by a receiver, abandoned replies closed as "stopped", an agent loop stopped after three, a too-fast agent slowed down.

15 · Later and optional: experiments still needed, shortcuts, larger size, open points

Experiments the draft needs
  • Done: the spine (E-003). Loss, repeats, order, outages, parking and rebuild all behaved as promised on a small build without PostgreSQL.
  • Done: the log on PostgreSQL (E-004). No change stepped over with numbering one writer at a time; a worker is woken in about 1 ms.
  • Done: the screen (E-005) and the agent door (E-003, scripted agents). Both contracts held under every interruption tried.
  • Done: rebuild at full size (E-003). 80 s for the feed at the seeded team's size, so Reset restores a saved copy instead.
  • Text rules judged by a model, already planned for last (D-001).

Streamed text needs no experiment: keeping it off the log is a design choice, proven by tests.

Shortcuts taken knowingly
  • No sign-in or permissions; a person switcher.
  • Search and notifications are stand-ins; the notification app stands for push.
  • One database server, with an area per part.
  • Log numbers handed out one at a time; the log is never trimmed.
  • One copy of each part: no spare if one fails.
  • A rebuild replays the whole log.
  • Agents may read every channel.

Full list with the proper answer for each: docs/known-shortcuts.md.

Also in the design, brieflyHow
A kept row whose message is deletedThe feed remembers for each row whether the reader kept it, a flag kept it, or both. Kept by the reader: the row stays in Focus as a marker until Done (D-004). Kept only by the flag: the delete removes the flag, so the row is released; read before, it stays among read rows as a marker; unread, it goes without a trace (D-011).
A flag removed after the row was readThe edit arrives with the whole message, now without the flag. The feed sees "kept by flag" no longer holds and moves the row to the read rows; a row the reader also kept stays.
A reaction from someone you addressedA reaction is a change to the message and carries all its reactions. The feed checks whether the reactor was mentioned, flagged or the other side of a direct conversation: then a row in Needs you, "Marco reacted ✅ to your message", and a notification. Anyone else: the grouped row in For your information with the split by emoji. When the last reaction goes, the row is retracted like a deleted message (D-014, D-004).
The control panelIts own program. It holds the schedule of what the scripted team will do, shows it ahead of time, plays it at a chosen pace, starts any seeded situation on demand, and acts only through the two doors. It also holds the switches on each stand-in: slow, refuse, off.
The seeded teamTwo weeks of history written by a fixed script (hand-written situations, generated background), loaded into Conversations as truth with times counted back from now. The receivers fill themselves from the log. The same script every time, so Reset gives the same start.
History tabsAnswered by the feed from its own copy, so they show the current truth as of the feed's place in the log; when the feed is behind they carry the same "Catching up" line.
Small product questions the draft ran into
  • A read row whose message is deleted keeps a marker (D-004), but the history tabs show "the current truth" and are meant to replace the Earlier list (D-016, the agent's reading). Where does the marker live? To settle when the mock-ups are reviewed.
  • An agent's reply closed as "stopped": does it reach anyone's feed, and with which level?
  • A notification already in the notification app whose row then leaves the bucket because of a dial change: what does the entry show?
  • A notification that would arrive more than ten minutes late: still an interruption, or only a entry marked "delayed"?
