Product

An activity feed that protects attention

When agents write as much as people, attention becomes the scarce resource. My bet: people will trust a feed that sorts by plain, visible rules and lets them shape what comes first.

How I approached it

  1. Picked an audienceStartup teams of 30+, mixed roles, heavy AI use
  2. Studied productsSlack, Teams, Linear, GitHub, Zulip, Mattermost
  3. Chose one betEvery later decision had to serve it
  4. Modelled the rulesA simulation exposed gaps before any UI
  5. Tried three designsClickable prototypes at desktop and phone width

What deserves someone's attention, and what doesn't

Focus · notifies

Anna: need your call on pricing todayFlagged for you · stays until Done
Deployer: waiting for approval to shipAgent stopped and flagged you

Needs you · notifies

Marco: @Maya can you check this?Mentioned you in #launch

For your information · silent

🎉 3 · 👍 2 on your announcementReactions to your message

Illustration based on the seeded team.

  • Focus is yours: your rules for people, channels and threads, plus flags that name you.
  • Needs you is directed at you: mentions, replies in your threads, and answers given by reaction.
  • For your information concerns you but isn't waiting on you. It never interrupts.
  • Doesn't reach you: ordinary channel traffic you didn't ask for, and agent text still being written.
  • Both sides shape it: senders mark Flag or Info, readers set a dial per channel and thread.
  • Every row explains itself, with one click to change the rule.
  • Reading clears a row. Keep holds what you still need to handle.

And where AI changed my mind

I first wanted direct mentions in Focus. The AI pushed back: many are a courtesy ("thanks @sam"), so Focus would become Needs you under another name. I agreed, and made it a one-click rule.

The ideas I brought to the product

  • Focus: a top bucket each person owns.
  • Flag and Info: senders set a message's weight.
  • Keep: triage effort only where it's needed.
  • Nothing jumps when messages change.
  • Reactions as answers, as teams really respond.
  • History tabs, kept apart from triage.
Parked · not in this build

Next bet: Focus rules in plain language, powered by an LLM

The valueExact rules can't express "anything about pricing or contracts". A language model could, so people could say what matters in their own words.
The safe designThe model could only lift an item into Focus, never hide or lower it. It runs after the item is in the feed, so a slow or failed model breaks nothing.
Why I parked itIt is a second bet, and a wrong call in the top bucket costs the most trust. So it is not in the product: I built the rule-based feed first and kept this design on record.

The market is heading there. Slack calls "intelligent prioritization" the next phase of Activity, Outlook's Copilot is rolling out priorities based on your own criteria, and Gmail is testing an AI Inbox. Contexia's angle would be rules you can see and own, which can only raise an item.

Technology

Reliable delivery to independent systems

Every message, edit and delete must reach search, notifications and the feed, even when one of them is slow or down.

How messages make their way to the activity feed

A change is saved together with a numbered log entry. A delivery worker reads the log and calls the feed, which applies changes strictly in order and works out each reader's row. Open screens get the result live and catch up by number after a disconnect.

Explore it: the clickable system map · what can fail · how notifications decide

What guarantees it provides

  • Nothing saved is lost. The change and its log entry commit together.
  • Repeats are harmless. Delivery is at least once, and receivers ignore repeats and older versions.
  • Failures stay contained. Each receiver has its own lane and position, and retries with backoff.
  • Bad changes are set aside after three refusals, visibly, and can be resent.
  • A notification goes out once, after a 5-second hold that lets a read or delete cancel it.
  • The feed can be rebuilt from the log, because it holds nothing original.

Where I knowingly cut corners

  • Sign-in, permissions and private channels.
  • A database standby: PostgreSQL is a single point of failure.
  • Real search and push providers, which sit behind the same contracts.
  • Old data is never trimmed.
  • Load testing. Capacity of about 5,000 people per organization is an estimate from analysis, not a measurement.

The key trade-offs

QuestionI choseOverBecause
How changes travelA log in PostgreSQL, saved with the changeA message brokerNo outside consumers yet, and one system holds the truth
Delivery promiseAt least once, with safe repeatsClaiming exactly onceNetworks repeat, so receivers must cope anyway
Who decides to notifyThe feedThe senderOnly the feed knows buckets, dials and what was read
Feed storageEach reader's rows computed on writeComputing at read timeFast to open, and simple at team size
OrderingOne log per organizationOne log per conversationSimple strict order, with a clear next step

How it grows

Parts without memory scale by adding copies. Organizations spread across databases, and a large one moves to parallel conversations and then a log per conversation. A broker becomes right when other teams consume the changes or traffic reaches the high hundreds of changes a second. Because receivers already ignore repeats, adding one replaces a single layer.

Where I overbuilt: the agent door and the multi-tenant lifecycle go beyond the brief. I knew that when I built them and let curiosity win. The organization in every key earns its place, and the rest could have stayed a documented path.
